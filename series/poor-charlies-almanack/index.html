Why are people more willing to obey justified commands?

You must clearly state who will do what, when, where, and why. If you write a note to a subordinate instructing them to do something but fail to explain the reason, Brown might fire you because he is very clear that an idea is most easily accepted only when its reasons are meticulously laid out. Unfortunately, this tendency to value reasons is so strong that even if the reason provided is meaningless or inaccurate, it can make one’s commands and requests easier to comply with. A psychological experiment has proven this. In this experiment, the researcher successfully cut in front of a long line waiting for a copier, giving the reason: “I need to copy a few things.” This unfortunate side effect of the tendency to value reasons is actually a conditioned reflex. This conditioned reflex occurs because most people believe that things with reasons are important. Naturally, certain businesses and cults often utilize various catchy/sensational reasons to achieve their hidden objectives.
