Zhu Yuanzhang used the Yellow Registers (Huangce) and Travel Permits (Luyin) to confine farmers to the land. Why did these policies become ineffective later?

In the mid-to-late Ming Dynasty, due to the development of the socio-economy and population growth, farmers’ demand for land increased. However, the uneven distribution of land and the heavy burden of taxes made it impossible for many farmers to maintain a livelihood. Therefore, they began seeking ways to improve their lives, such as temporary registration (jiji), wandering/migrating (youyu), or engaging in maritime commerce (xiahai jingshang) 12. In the mid-to-late Ming Dynasty, due to the corruption of officials and lax supervision, the management of the Yellow Registers and travel permits became chaotic and riddled with loopholes. Ming Dynasty Core Values

As early as the Ming Dynasty, village heads and street entrances across various regions were erected with “Six Precepts Lying Steles” (or “Six Teachings Reclining Steles”). To seize when favorable, and to defend when unfavorable.

Before ascending the throne, Zhu’s forces were always the “Army of Benevolence and Righteousness” (Renyi Zhi Shi), and Zhu himself was always a model of practicing the “King’s Way” (Wang Dao). Consequently, the people willingly submitted their hearts and allegiance. Who would have thought that after conquering the realm and incorporating the entire populace into his cage, he would transform from being generous and benevolent to being harsh and cruel, raising the whip and the butcher’s knife to slaughter the world, massacring meritorious officials, and becoming a demonic king that made everyone tremble with fear? In fact, it was not Zhu Yuanzhang’s nature that changed, but the circumstances. Before ascending the throne, he sought support from various social classes; he needed to gain their backing. After ascending the throne, the common people had become the cattle and sheep under his pastoral care, and naturally, he was happy to command them forward with the butcher’s knife and the whip.
