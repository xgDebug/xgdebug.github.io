财富的简单之路

这篇文章提供了一份关于实现财务独立的综合指南，强调了避免债务、明智投资和保持个人理财纪律性的重要性。它就长期投资股票市场的益处、试图择时市场的风险，以及为了积累财富而“节制开支”（过着低于收入的生活）的价值提供了建议。

Beginnings#

  • “If you reach for a star, you might not get one. But you won’t come up with a hand full of mud either.” （如果你伸手去抓一颗星星，你可能抓不到。但你也不会抓到一捧泥巴。）

  • Spend less than you earn—invest the surplus—avoid debt. （花得比赚得少——投资盈余——避免债务。）

  • Carrying debt is as appealing as being covered with leeches and has much the same effect. （背负债务就像被吸满寄生虫一样诱人，而且效果也大同小异。）

  • If your lifestyle matches—or god forbid exceeds—your income, you are no more than a gilded slave. （如果你的生活方式与你的收入相匹配——或者天哪，超过了你的收入——你不过是一个镀金的奴隶。）

  • Avoid investment advisors. Too many have only their own interests at heart. By the time you know enough to pick a good one, you know enough to handle your finances yourself. It’s your money and no one will care for it better than you. （避开投资顾问。太多人只顾自己的利益。等你了解得足够多，能挑选到一个好的顾问时，你就已经了解得足够多，可以自己管理自己的财务了。这是你的钱，没有人会比你自己更关心它。）

  • Money can buy many things, but nothing more valuable than your freedom. （金钱可以买到很多东西，但没有什么比你的自由更宝贵的了。）

  • Life choices are not always about the money, but you should always be clear about the financial impact of the choices you make. （生活选择不总是关于金钱的，但你永远应该清楚你所做的选择所带来的财务影响。）

  • Try saving and investing 50% of your income. With no debt, this is perfectly doable. （尝试储蓄和投资你收入的 50%。在没有债务的情况下，这完全是可行的。）

  • The stock market is a powerful wealth-building tool and you should be investing in it. But realize the market and the value of your shares will sometimes drop dramatically. This is absolutely normal and to be expected. When it happens, ignore the drops and buy more shares. （股市是一个强大的财富积累工具，你应该投资于它。但要明白，市场和你的股票价值有时会大幅下跌。这是绝对正常且可以预期的。当这种情况发生时，忽略这些下跌，买入更多股票。）

  • 股市暴跌是必然的,高度确定的.就像天会下雨一样,不可避免的,那是你买入的机会 （股市暴跌是必然的，高度确定的。就像天会下雨一样，不可避免的，那是你买入的机会。）

  • This will be much, much harder than you think. People all around you will panic. The news media will be screaming Sell, Sell, Sell! （这会比你想象的要困难得多。你周围的人都会恐慌。新闻媒体会尖叫着喊着“卖！卖！卖！”）

  • When you can live on 4% of your investments per year, you are financially independent. （当你每年能从你的投资中获得 4% 的收入来生活时，你就实现了财务独立。）

  • “You know, if you could learn to cater to the king, you wouldn’t have to live on rice and beans.” To which the monk replies: “If you could learn to live on rice and beans, you wouldn’t have to cater to the king.” （“你知道，如果你能学会为国王服务，你就不用靠米和豆子生活了。” 僧侣回答道：“如果你能学会靠米和豆子生活，你就不用为国王服务了。”）

Part I: Orientation#

  • If you intend to achieve financial freedom, you are going to have to think differently. It starts by recognizing that debt should not be considered normal. It should be recognized as the vicious, pernicious destroyer of wealth-building potential it truly is. It has no place in your financial life. （如果你打算实现财务自由，你就必须以不同的方式思考。它始于认识到债务不应该被视为常态。它应该被视为一个恶毒的、有害的、摧毁财富积累潜力的存在。它在你的财务生活中没有立足之地。）

  • Your lifestyle is diminished. Set aside any aspirations to financial freedom. Even if your goal is living the maximum consumer lifestyle, the more debt you carry the more of your income is devoured by interest payments. A (sometimes huge) portion of your income has already been spent. （你的生活方式受到了限制。抛开任何对财务自由的憧憬。即使你的目标是过上最极致的消费生活，你背负的债务越多，你的收入就越被利息支付所吞噬。你收入的一部分（有时是巨大的部分）已经被花掉了。）

  • You are enslaved to whatever source of income you have. Your debt needs to be serviced. Your practical ability to make choices congruent with your values and long-term goals is seriously constrained. Your stress levels build. It feels as if you are being buried alive. The emotional and psychological effects of being saddled with debt are real and dangerous. You endure the same type of negative emotions experienced by any addict: shame, guilt, loneliness, and above all, helplessness. The fact that it’s a prison of your own making makes it all the more difficult. Your options can become so narrowed and your stress levels so high, you risk turning to self-destructive patterns that only reinforce the dependence on spending. Drinking perhaps, or smoking. Or, ironically, shopping and still more spending. It’s a dangerous, self-perpetuating cycle. Your debt tends to focus your attention on the past, present and future exclusively in the worst possible way. You become fixated on your past mistakes, your present pain and the disaster looming ahead. Your brain tends to shut down on the subject with the vague hope it will all resolve itself in some magical way and in the magical time of later. Living with debt becomes hardwired in your financial attitudes, habits and values. （你被你所拥有的任何收入来源所奴役。你的债务需要被偿还。你做出符合你的价值观和长期目标的选择的能力受到了严重的限制。你的压力水平不断增加。感觉就像你正在被活埋。背负债务带来的情感和心理影响是真实且危险的。你承受着任何成瘾者都会经历的同一种负面情绪：羞耻、内疚、孤独，以及最重要的是，无助。它是一个你自己制造的监狱，这让一切变得更加困难。你的选择可能会变得如此狭窄，你的压力水平可能会变得如此之高，以至于你可能会转向自我毁灭的模式，从而加剧对消费的依赖。也许是饮酒，或者吸烟。或者，讽刺的是，购物和更多的消费。这是一个危险的、自我延续的循环。你的债务倾向于将你的注意力集中在过去、现在和未来上，而且是以最糟糕的方式。你开始沉迷于过去的错误、现在的痛苦和迫在眉睫的灾难。你的大脑倾向于对这个话题关闭，带着一种模糊的希望，即所有事情都会以某种神奇的方式、在某个神奇的未来时间解决。与债务共存会固化到你的财务态度、习惯和价值观中。）

  • If your interest rate is… Less than 3%, pay it off slowly and route the money to your investments instead. Between 3-5%, do whatever feels most comfortable: Either put the money to debt payment or investments. More than 5%, pay it off ASAP. （如果你的利率是……低于 3%，慢慢偿还，并将这笔钱导向你的投资。在 3-5% 之间，做任何让你感觉最舒适的事情：要么用于偿还债务，要么用于投资。高于 5%，尽快偿还。）

  • If your goal is financial independence, it is also to hold as little debt as possible. This means you’ll seek the least house to meet your needs rather than the most house you can technically afford. （如果你的目标是财务独立，那么也应该持有尽可能少的债务。这意味着你会寻找最少的房屋来满足你的需求，而不是你能负担得起的最多房屋。）

  • More house also means more stuff to maintain and fill it. The more and greater things you allow in your life, the more of your time, money and life energy they demand. （更多的房屋也意味着需要维护和填充更多的东西。你允许进入你生活的东西越多，它们就越需要消耗你的时间、金钱和生命能量。）

  • Houses are an expensive indulgence, not an investment. That’s OK if and when the time for such an indulgence comes. I’ve owned them myself. But don’t let yourself be blinded by the idea that owning one is necessary, always financially sound and automatically justifies taking on this “good debt.” （房屋是一种昂贵的奢侈品，而不是一项投资。如果到了需要这种奢侈品的时候，那没关系。我自己就拥有过。但不要让“拥有房屋是必要的、永远财务稳健的、自动证明了承担这种‘良性债务’的”这个想法让你盲目。）

  • Money is a very relative thing. （金钱是一个非常相对的概念。）

  • Money can buy many things, but none of which is more important than your financial independence. （金钱可以买到很多东西，但没有哪一样比你的财务独立更重要的。）

  • Stop thinking about what your money can buy. Start thinking about what your money can earn. And then think about what the money it earns can earn. （停止思考你的钱能买到什么。开始思考你的钱能赚到什么。然后思考它赚到的钱又能赚到什么。）

  • Warren Buffett is rather famously quoted as saying: Rule #1: Never lose money. Rule #2: Never forget rule #1. Unfortunately, too many people take this at face value and leap to the conclusion that Mr. Buffett has found a magical way to dance in and out of the market, avoiding the inevitable drops. This is not true and in fact he is on record speaking to the folly of trying: “The Dow started the last century at 66 and ended at 11,400. How could you lose money during a period like that? A lot of people did because they tried to dance in and out.” （沃伦·巴菲特被引述说得相当有名：规则 #1：永远不要亏钱。规则 #2：永远不要忘记规则 #1。不幸的是，太多人只是表面上接受了这些话，就跳到了结论：巴菲特找到了一个神奇的方法，可以在市场中进进出出，避开不可避免的下跌。这并非事实，事实上，他曾公开谈论这种尝试的愚蠢之处：“道琼斯指数上世纪初是 66，结束时是 11,400。在那种时期，你怎么会亏钱呢？很多人亏了，因为他们试图进进出出。”）

  • Like the rest of us, Buffett was unable to time the market and in fact, knowing market timing to be a fool’s errand, he didn’t even try. But unlike many others, Buffett didn’t panic and sell. He knew that such events are to be expected. In fact, he continued to invest as the sharp decline offered new opportunities. When the market recovered, as it always does, so did his fortune. So did the fortunes of all who stayed the course. That’s why I put “lost” in quotes. （和我们其他人一样，巴菲特也无法精准预测市场时机，事实上，他知道市场时机预测是一件愚蠢的事情，所以他甚至没有尝试。但与许多人不同，巴菲特没有恐慌并抛售。他知道这样的事件是预料之中的。事实上，当市场急剧下跌时，他继续投资，因为这种下跌提供了新的机会。当市场像往常一样复苏时，他的财富也随之复苏。所有坚持下去的人的财富也是如此。这就是为什么我把“亏损”放在了引号里。）

  • At $ 56 per share or at $ 52 per share, you still own the same 186.3238308 shares of VTSAX. That in turns means you own a piece of virtually every publicly traded company in the U.S.— （无论是以每股 56 美元还是 52 美元，你仍然拥有相同的 186,323,830.8 股 VTSAX。这意味着你拥有了美国几乎每一家公开交易公司的股份——）

  • Once you truly understand this, you’ll begin to realize that in owning VTSAX you are tying your financial future to that same large, diverse group of companies based in the most powerful, wealthiest and most influential country on the planet. These companies are filled with hardworking people focused on prospering in the changing world around them and dealing with all the uncertainties it can create. （一旦你真正理解了这一点，你就会开始意识到，拥有 VTSAX 意味着你将自己的财务未来与同一批总部位于地球上最强大、最富有、最具影响力的国家的庞大、多元化的公司联系起来。这些公司里充满了勤奋的人们，他们专注于在周围不断变化的世界中繁荣发展，并应对它可能带来的所有不确定性。）

  • 你拥有的是公司的一部分，拥有了全世界最聪明的大脑帮你工作。你是他们的老板。 （你拥有的是公司的一部分，拥有了全世界最聪明的大脑帮你工作。你是他们的老板。）

  • It is simply not possible to time the market, regardless of all the heavily credentialed gurus on CNBC and the like who claim they can. The market is the most powerful wealth-building tool of all time. The market always goes up and it is always a wild and rocky ride along the way. Since we can’t predict these swings, we need to toughen up mentally and ride them out. I want my money working as hard as possible, as soon as possible. （无论 CNBC 上那些拥有大量资历的“大师”声称他们能做到什么，精准预测市场时机都是不可能的。市场是史上最强大的财富积累工具。市场总是上涨的，而且沿途总是是一段狂野而崎岖的旅程。既然我们无法预测这些波动，我们就需要在精神上变得坚强，并经历它们。我希望我的钱能尽快、尽可能努力地工作。）

  • Fear is perfectly understandable. Nobody wants to lose money. But until you master it, such fear will be deadly to your wealth. It will prevent you from investing. Once you are invested, it will cause you to flee in panic for the exits every time the market drops. And drop—repeatedly on its relentless march upward—it will. The curse of fear is that it will drive you to panic and sell when you should be holding. The market is volatile. Crashes, pullbacks and corrections are all absolutely normal. None of them are the end of the world, and none are even the end of the market’s relentless rise. They are all, each and every one, expected parts of the process. （恐惧是完全可以理解的。没有人想亏钱。但直到你掌握了它，这种恐惧就会对你的财富致命。它会阻止你投资。一旦你投资了，它就会让你在市场每次下跌时都恐慌性地逃离，寻找出口。而且它会下跌——在它不懈的向上行进中反复下跌——它一定会下跌。恐惧的诅咒在于，它会驱使你恐慌并抛售，而此时你应该持有。市场是波动的。崩盘、回调和修正都是绝对正常的。没有一个代表着世界的终结，甚至没有一个代表着市场不懈上涨的终结。它们都是这个过程中预期的组成部分，每一个都是。）

  • Any investing done short term is by definition speculation. （任何短期投资，从定义上来说，都是投机。）

  • 任何短期行为都是投机 （任何短期行为都是投机。）

  • Therefore, if we know a crash is coming, why not wait to invest? Or, if currently invested why not sell, wait till the fall and then go back in? The answer is simply because we don’t know when the crash will occur or end. Nobody does. （因此，如果我们知道崩盘即将来临，为什么不等待投资呢？或者，如果目前已经投资了，为什么不卖出，等待下跌后再重新买入呢？答案很简单，因为我们不知道崩盘会在何时发生或结束。没有人知道。）

Part II: How to harness the world’s most powerful wealth-building tool#

  • “Simplicity is the keynote of all true elegance.” （“简单是所有真正优雅的基调。”）

  • “Those who would trade liberty for security deserve neither.” （“那些愿意用自由来换取安全感的人，两者都配不上。”）

  • The market always recovers. Always. And, if someday it really doesn’t, no investment will be safe and none of this financial stuff will matter anyway. （市场总是会复苏的。永远如此。而且，如果有一天它真的没有复苏，那么没有任何投资是安全的，而且这一切的财务知识也都不重要了。）

  • 世界崩坏,找个墓地吧 （世界崩坏，找个墓地吧。）

  • The market always goes up. Always. （市场总是上涨的。永远如此。）

  • 区别在于是不是当权者拿走你那份 （区别在于是不是当权者拿走你那份。）

  • The market is the single best performing investment class over time, bar none. （从长远来看，市场是表现最好的投资类别，无可匹敌。）

  • The next 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 years will have just as many collapses, recessions and disasters as in the past. Like the good Professor says, it’s not possible to prevent them. Every time this happens your investments will take a hit. Every time it will be scary as hell. Every time all the smart guys will be screaming: Sell!! And every time only those few with enough nerve will stay the course and prosper. （接下来的 10、20、30、40、50 年，将拥有与过去一样多的崩溃、衰退和灾难。正如那位好教授所说，这是无法避免的。每当这种情况发生，你的投资就会受到冲击。每一次都会是极度可怕的。每一次所有聪明人都将尖叫着喊：卖！而每一次只有那些有足够胆量的人才能坚持下去并繁荣昌盛。）

    1. This is why you have to toughen up, learn to ignore the noise and ride out the storm; adding still more money to your investments as you go. （6. 这就是为什么你必须变得坚强，学会忽略噪音并乘风破浪；同时不断地向你的投资中投入更多的资金。）
    1. To be strong enough to stay the course you need to know these bad things are coming—not only intellectually but on an emotional level as well. You need to know this deep in your gut. They will happen. They will hurt. But like blizzards in winter they should never be a surprise. And, unless you panic, they won’t matter. （7. 为了有足够的力量坚持下去，你需要知道这些坏事正在发生——不仅是智力上的，也是情感层面的。你需要深信不疑。它们会发生。它们会带来痛苦。但就像冬季的暴风雪一样，它们不应该成为惊喜。而且，除非你恐慌，否则它们就不会重要。）
    1. There’s a major market crash coming!! And there’ll be another after that!! What wonderful buying opportunities they’ll be. （8. 一场重大的市场崩盘即将来临！！之后还会还有另一场！！那将是多么美妙的买入机会啊。）

  • Of course, over those same years she’s going to see several major bull markets as well. Some will rage beyond all reason, along with the hype that will surround them. When those occur, the financial media will declare “this time it’s different” with all the same confidence as when they claimed the end had come. In this too they will be wrong. （当然，在同一几年里，她还会看到好几次重大的牛市。有些牛市会超出所有理性的程度而爆发，伴随着围绕它们的炒作。当这些牛市发生时，金融媒体会像他们宣布末日时一样，带着同样的信心宣布“这一次不一样”。在这方面，他们也会是错误的。）

  • Through disaster after disaster the market always makes its way higher over time. It’s a wild ride along the way. There is a Big, Ugly Event. （历经一场灾难又一场灾难，市场总是在不断向上发展。这是一段狂野的旅程。存在着一个巨大而丑陋的事件。）

    1. The market is self-cleansing. （1. 市场是自我净化的。）
    1. Owning stock is owning a part of living, breathing, dynamic companies, each striving to succeed. （2. 拥有股票就是拥有一个正在生存、呼吸、动态的公司的一部分，每个公司都在努力取得成功。）

  • Stocks are not just little slips of traded paper. When you own stock you own a piece of a business. These are companies filled with people working endlessly to expand and serve their customer base. They are competing in an unforgiving environment that rewards those who can make it happen and discards those who can’t. It is this intense dynamic that makes stocks and the companies they represent the most powerful and successful investment class in history. （股票不仅仅是小小的交易纸片。当你拥有股票时，你拥有的是一家企业的一部分。这些公司里充满了人们，他们不知疲倦地工作，以扩展业务并服务于他们的客户群。它们在一个不容妥协的环境中竞争，奖励那些能够实现目标的人，淘汰那些做不到的人。正是这种激烈的动态，使得股票和它们所代表的公司成为历史上最强大、最成功的投资类别。）

  • Why most people lose money in the market （为什么大多数人会在市场中亏钱）

  • Most people lose money in the stock market. （大多数人在股市中亏钱。）

    1. We think we can time the market. （1. 我们认为我们可以预测市场时机。）
    1. We believe we can pick individual stocks. （2. 我们相信我们可以挑选出个股。）
    1. We believe we can pick winning mutual fund managers. （3. 我们相信我们可以挑选出获胜的共同基金经理。）
    1. We focus on the foam. （4. 我们关注泡沫。）

  • It is the beer: The actual operating businesses of which we can own a part. It is the foam: The traded pieces of paper that furiously rise and fall in price from moment-to-moment. This is the market of CNBC. （真正的“啤酒”：是我们能够拥有股份的实际运营企业。而“泡沫”：是那些在价格上从一刻到下一刻疯狂上涨和下跌的交易纸片。这就是 CNBC 的市场。）

  • The good news for our VTSAX wealth-building strategy (which we’ll discuss in depth in the next few chapters) is that stocks are a pretty good inflation hedge. As we’ve discussed, in owning stocks we own businesses. These businesses have assets and create products. The value of those rise with inflation, providing a hedge against the falling value of the currency. This is especially true in times of low to moderate inflation. （对于我们的 VTSAX 财富积累策略（我们将在接下来的几章中深入讨论）来说，一个好消息是：股票是一个相当好的通胀对冲工具。正如我们所讨论的，拥有股票就是拥有企业。这些企业拥有资产并创造产品。这些资产的价值会随着通货膨胀而上升，为货币价值的下降提供对冲。这在低到中等通胀时期尤为真实。）

  • In what stage of your investing life are you? The Wealth Accumulation Stage or the Wealth Preservation Stage? Or perhaps a blend of the two? What level of risk do you find acceptable? Is your investment horizon long-term or short-term? （你在投资生涯的哪个阶段？是财富积累阶段还是财富保值阶段？或者两者兼有？你认为可接受的风险水平是多少？你的投资期限是长期还是短期？）

  • Safety is a bit of an illusion. （安全感只是一种错觉。）

    1. To buy the index is to accept the market’s “average” return. People have trouble accepting the idea of themselves or anything in their life as average. （2. 买入指数意味着接受市场的“平均”回报。人们很难接受自己或生活中任何事情是“平均”的这个概念。）

  • Bonds pay interest, providing us with an income flow. （债券支付利息，为我们提供现金流。）

  • When you buy stock you are buying a part ownership in a company. When you buy bonds you are loaning money to a company or government agency. （当你买股票时，你是在购买一家公司的一部分所有权。当你买债券时，你是在向一家公司或政府机构借钱。）

  • Since deflation occurs when the price of stuff falls, when the money you’ve lent is paid back, it has more purchasing power. Your money buys more stuff than when you lent it. This increase in value helps to offset the losses deflation will bring to your other assets. In times of inflation prices rise and so money owed to you loses value. When you get paid back your cash buys less stuff. Then it is better to own assets, like stocks, that rise in value with inflation. （由于通货紧缩发生在商品价格下跌时，当你借出的钱被偿还时，它就拥有了更大的购买力。你的钱比你借出时能买到更多的东西。这种价值的增加有助于抵消通货紧缩给你的其他资产带来的损失。在通货膨胀时期，价格上涨，因此欠你的钱的价值就会下降。当你得到偿还时，你的现金能买到的东西就更少了。那么，拥有像股票这样随通胀而升值的资产就更好。）

  • The two key elements of bonds are the interest rate and the term. （债券的两个关键要素是利率和期限。）

  • 流动性与收益率 （流动性与收益率）

  • When interest rates rise, bond prices fall. When interest rates fall, bond prices rise. In either case, if you hold a bond to the end of its term you will, barring default, get exactly what you paid for it. （当利率上升时，债券价格下跌。当利率下降时，债券价格上涨。无论哪种情况，如果你持有债券直到其期限结束，在没有违约的情况下，你将获得你购买时的确切金额。）

  • when we get an Inverted Yield Curve and short-term rates are higher than long-term rates, investors are anticipating low inflation or even deflation. （当我们出现倒挂收益率曲线，短期利率高于长期利率时，投资者正在预期低通胀甚至通货紧缩。）

  • Put all your eggs in one basket and forget about it. （把所有鸡蛋放在一个篮子里，然后忘记它。）

  • The great irony of investing is that the more you watch and fiddle with your holdings the less well you are likely to do. Fill your basket, add as much as you can along the way and ignore it the rest of the time. You’ll likely wake up rich. （投资的巨大讽刺在于，你越是关注和摆弄你的持仓，你成功的可能性就越低。把你的篮子装满，在过程中尽可能多地添加，然后在其余时间忽略它。你很可能会醒来时发现自己是富翁。）

  • Owning 100% stocks like this is considered a very aggressive investment allocation. It is aggressive and in this Wealth Accumulation Phase, you should be. You have decades ahead and you’ll be adding new money as you go. Market ups and downs don’t matter because you’ll avoid panic and stay the course. If anything, you recognize drops as the “stocks on sale” buying opportunities they are. Perhaps 40 years from now (or whenever you are living on your portfolio) you might want to add a bond index fund to smooth the ride. Worry about that then. （像这样拥有 100% 股票被认为是一种非常激进的投资配置。它是激进的，而在财富积累阶段，你就应该如此。你还有几十年时间，而且你会不断地投入新资金。市场涨跌并不重要，因为你会避免恐慌并坚持下去。如果非要说，你会将下跌视为“打折股票”的买入机会。也许 40 年后（或者当你靠投资组合生活时），你可能会想增加一个债券指数基金来平稳旅程。届时再为此担心。）

  • You can’t predict them and you can’t time them. （你无法预测它们，也无法精准预测它们的时间。）

  • If you look at all asset classes from bonds to real estate to gold to farmland to art to racehorses to whatever, stocks provide the best performance over time. Nothing else even comes close. （如果你看所有资产类别，从债券到房地产、黄金、农田、艺术品、赛马到任何东西，股票在长期表现上提供了最好的回报。其他任何东西都无法与之匹敌。）

  • Stocks are not just little slips of traded paper. When you own stock you own a piece of a business. Many of these have extensive international operations, allowing you to participate in all the markets across the globe. （股票不仅仅是小小的交易纸片。当你拥有股票时，你拥有的是一家企业的一部分。其中许多公司拥有广泛的国际业务，使你能够参与到全球所有的市场中。）

  • Because VTSAX is an index fund, we don’t even have to worry about which companies will succeed and which will fail. As we’ve seen, it is ‘self-cleansing.’ The failures fall away and the winners can grow endlessly. （因为 VTSAX 是一个指数基金，我们甚至不必担心哪些公司会成功，哪些会失败。正如我们所看到的，它是“自我净化的”。失败者会淘汰，而赢家可以无限成长。）

  • Put all your eggs into one large and diverse basket, add more whenever you can and forget about it. The more you add the faster you’ll get there. Job done. （把所有鸡蛋放入一个大型且多元化的篮子里，每当你可以时就添加更多，然后忘记它。你添加得越多，到达目标的速度就越快。任务完成。）

  • ~ 75% Stocks: VTSAX (Vanguard Total Stock Market Index Fund). Still our core holding for all the reasons we’ve discussed. ~ 20% Bonds: VBTLX (Vanguard Total Bond Market Index Fund). Bonds provide some income, tend to smooth out the rough ride of stocks and are a deflation hedge. ~ 5% Cash: We hold ours in our local bank. （~ 75% 股票：VTSAX（先锋全股票市场指数基金）。根据我们讨论的所有原因，这仍然是我们的核心持仓。~ 20% 债券：VBTLX（先锋全债券市场指数基金）。债券提供一些收入，倾向于平稳股票的剧烈波动，并且是通缩对冲工具。~ 5% 现金：我们将其存放在本地银行。）

  • Life is balance and choice. Add more of this, lose a little of that. （生活是平衡与选择。增加更多这个，损失一点那个。）

  • The wealth accumulation stage is when you are working and have earned income to save and invest. For this stage I favor 100% stocks and VTSAX is the fund I prefer. If financial independence is your goal, your savings rate in these years should be high. As you invest that money each month it serves to smooth out the market’s wild ride. You enter the wealth preservation stage once you step away from your job and regular paychecks and begin living on income from your investments. At this point, I recommend adding bonds to the portfolio. Like the fresh cash you were investing while working, bonds help smooth the ride. （财富积累阶段是你工作并有收入可以储蓄和投资的阶段。对于这个阶段，我倾向于 100% 股票，而 VTSAX 是我偏好的基金。如果财务独立是你的目标，你在这些年份的储蓄率应该很高。当你每月投资这些资金时，它有助于平稳市场的狂野波动。当你离开工作和定期工资，开始依靠投资收入生活时，你就进入了财富保值阶段。在这个阶段，我建议将债券添加到投资组合中。就像你在工作时投入的新现金一样，债券有助于平稳旅程。）

  • Managing other people’s money is a very big business, and for those who engage in it, a very lucrative one. （管理别人的钱是一项非常大的生意，对于从事这项业务的人来说，它也是一项非常有利可图的生意。）

Part III: Magic Beans#

  • Buffett writes: “My advice … could not be more simple: Put 10% of the cash in short-term government bonds and 90% in a very low-cost S&P 500 index fund. (I suggest Vanguard’s.) I believe the trust’s long-term results from this policy will be superior to those attained by most investors–whether pension funds, institutions or individuals–who employ high-fee managers.” （巴菲特写道：“我的建议……不能比这更简单了：将 10% 的现金投入短期政府债券，90% 投入到成本极低的标普 500 指数基金中。（我建议是先锋的。）我相信，该信托基金根据这项政策取得的长期业绩，将优于大多数投资者——无论是养老基金、机构还是个人——所采用的高费率经理所取得的业绩。”）

  • My advice: Use the index funds and the company Mr. Bogle created and keep what is yours. （我的建议：使用指数基金，使用伯格尔先生创建的公司，并保留属于你自己的那部分。）

  • At this point you are probably beginning to see why I’m not a DCA fan, but let’s list the reasons anyway: By dollar cost averaging you are betting that the market will drop, saving yourself some pain. For any given year the odds of this happening are only ~ 23%. But the market is about 77% more likely to rise, in which case you will have spared yourself some gain. With each new invested portion you’ll be paying more for your shares. When you DCA you are basically saying the market is too high to invest all at once. In other words, you have strayed into the murky world of market timing. Which, as we’ve discussed, is a loser’s game. DCAing screws with your asset allocation. In the beginning you will be holding an outsized allocation of cash sitting on the sidelines waiting to be deployed. That’s OK if that’s your allocation strategy. But if not you need to understand that, in choosing to DCA, you’ve changed your allocation in a deep and fundamental way. When choosing to DCA, you must also choose the time horizon. Since the market tends to rise over time, if you choose a long horizon, say over a year, you increase the risk of paying more for your shares while you are investing. If you choose a shorter period of time, you reduce the value of using DCA in the first place. Finally, once you reach the end of your DCA period and are fully invested, you run the same risk of the market plunging the day after you are done. （到这个阶段，你可能开始明白为什么我不是一个“定投”（DCA）的粉丝，但我们还是列出原因：通过美元成本平均法（DCA），你是在押注市场会下跌，从而避免了自己的一些痛苦。对于任何给定的年份，这种情况发生的概率只有约 23%。但市场上涨的可能性要高出约 77%，在这种情况下，你就能避免一些收益。随着每一笔新的投资，你都会为你的股票支付更高的价格。当你进行 DCA 时，你基本上是在说市场太高了，不应该一次性全部投资。换句话说，你已经踏入了市场择时的浑浊世界。正如我们所讨论的，这是一个输家游戏。DCA 会干扰你的资产配置。一开始，你将持有超额的现金配置，它在场边等待部署。如果这是你的配置策略，那没关系。但如果不是，你需要明白，选择 DCA，你已经从根本上、深刻地改变了你的配置。当你选择 DCA 时，你也必须选择时间范围。由于市场倾向于随时间上涨，如果你选择一个较长的周期，比如一年以上，你在投资期间支付更多股票的风险就会增加。如果你选择一个较短的周期，你就降低了使用 DCA 本身的价值。最后，一旦你达到了 DCA 期的终点并完全投资，你就面临着市场在你完成投资后的第二天就会暴跌的同样风险。）

  • Step 1: Make a prediction for a huge short-term swing in the market. Up or down doesn’t matter. But down is easier and scarier. Scarier will get you more play if you’re right. Step 2: Document the time and date you made it. Step 3: When it doesn’t happen, wait a bit. Step 4: Repeat Steps 1-3 until one day you’re right. Step 5: Issue Press Release: Market Plunges!!!, just as (insert your name here) recently predicted. Step 6: Clear your schedule for media interviews. Step 7: Send me my 15% agent’s fee of your new-found wealth. Be sure not to issue your press release until events prove you right. （步骤 1：对市场短期内的大幅波动做出预测。上涨还是下跌并不重要。但下跌更容易、更可怕。更可怕的预测，如果你是对的，会给你带来更多的回报。步骤 2：记录你做出预测的时间和日期。步骤 3：如果预测没有实现，就稍等片刻。步骤 4：重复步骤 1-3，直到有一天你猜对了。步骤 5：发布新闻稿：“市场暴跌！！”，就像（在此插入你的名字）最近所预测的那样。步骤 6：清空你的日程表，准备媒体采访。步骤 7：将你新发现财富的 15% 作为我的代理费寄给我。一定要确保在事件证明你是对的之前，不要发布你的新闻稿。）

  • Rule #1: Everybody can be conned. Certainly stupid people are marks. But so are the exceptionally bright. The moment you start to think that it can’t happen to you, you’ve become a most attractive target. The easiest victims are those that think they are too smart, too knowledgeable to be taken. This means you, bucko. （规则 #1：每个人都可能被骗。当然，愚蠢的人是容易上当的。但那些特别聪明的人也一样。当你开始认为“这不可能发生在我身上”的那一刻，你就成为了一个极具吸引力的目标。最容易受骗的受害者是那些认为自己太聪明、太有知识而不会被抓住的人。这意味着，老兄，就是你。）

  • Rule #2: You are likely to be conned in an area of your expertise. The reason is simple: Targeting and ego. When con men pick a scam they look for people to whom it will naturally appeal. Those are people in the field. People feel secure and safe in those areas they know well. They believe they will be too smart to be taken unawares. Smart people know the areas they don’t know and tend to be far more cautious there. Many of Bernie Madoff’s victims were financial professionals. （规则 #2：你很可能会在你的专业领域中被骗。原因很简单：目标锁定和自负。当骗子选择一个骗局时，他们会寻找那些自然会被这个骗局吸引的人。这些人就是该领域的专业人士。人们在他们熟悉的地方会感到安全和安心。他们相信自己会足够聪明，不会措手不及。聪明人知道自己不了解的领域，并且往往在那里会更加谨慎。伯尼·马多夫的许多受害者都是金融专业人士。）

  • Rule #3: Con men (and women) don’t look like con men. This isn’t the movies. They’re not going to have slouch hats pulled low over their shifty eyes. Successful con men look like the safest, most trustworthy, honest, stable, comforting people imaginable. You won’t see them coming. Or rather you will, and you’ll be warmly welcoming them. （规则 #3：骗子（和骗子）看起来不像骗子。这不是电影。他们不会戴着低垂的帽子遮住他们狡黠的眼睛。成功的骗子看起来像是你能想象到的最安全、最值得信赖、最诚实、最稳定、最令人安心的人。你不会察觉到他们的到来。或者更确切地说，你会察觉到，而且你会热情地欢迎他们。）

  • Rule #4: 99% of what they say will be true. The best, most effective lies are surrounded by truth. Buried in it. The con, the thing that will leave you broke and with a real reason to cry, is carefully hidden. It is deep in the proverbial fine print. （规则 #4：他们所说的话中，99% 将是真实的。最好的、最有效的谎言是围绕着真相的。深埋在真相之中。这个骗局，那个会让你破产并有充分理由哭泣的事情，被小心翼翼地隐藏起来了。它深藏在俗称的“细则”之中。）

  • Rule #5: If it looks too good to be true, it is. There is no free lunch. Not ever. Your Mama taught you this. She was right. Listen to your Mama. （规则 #5：如果看起来好得不像真的，那它就是真的。没有免费的午餐。永远没有。你的妈妈教你的。她是对的。听你的妈妈的。）

If/ when Social Security comes through, enjoy. （规划你的财务未来时，要假设社会保障（Social Security）不会为你提供支持。过着低于你收入的生活，投资盈余，避免债务，积累“去你的钱”（F-You Money）。在财务上和在其他方面都保持独立。如果/当社会保障到位时，就享受它。）
