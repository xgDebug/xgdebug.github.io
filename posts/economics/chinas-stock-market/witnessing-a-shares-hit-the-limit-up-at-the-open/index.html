Witnessing A-shares hit the limit up at the open

A-shares collectively surge! The Shanghai Composite Index opened up by over 10%, the ChiNext Board surged by 18.44%, and over a thousand stocks in the entire market rose by more than 19%.

A Day of Witnessing Miracles#

Today, the Shanghai Composite Index (SSE) opened 10% higher, but immediately slid downwards, ultimately closing up 4.59%, settling at 3489 points. The total trading volume of the two markets reached a massive 3.45 trillion yuan, setting a new historical high. What is particularly striking is that at the opening, almost all stocks touched the upper limit (limit-up), regardless of whether their performance was good or bad, or how high their P/E ratio was. This market trend can be described as a peculiar phenomenon unseen in decades.

Questions Behind the Miracle#

Behind this phenomenon, we cannot help but ask:

  1. Has the economy truly begun to recover?
  2. Has the business environment improved?
  3. Has investment started to accelerate?
  4. Has consumer willingness increased?

Clearly, the answer is no. The market frenzy does not stem from a substantial improvement in economic fundamentals, but rather appears to be the result of human intervention.

Has the Market Reached a Phase Peak?#

The market currently seems to have reached a phase peak, but this does not mean the upward trend will immediately end. In fact, the driving force behind this round of market activity is far more than just market sentiment; it is more complex, involving multiple economic and policy factors.

The Fundamental Reason for This Market Uptick#

Since the economy peaked in 2022, the Chinese economy has gradually entered a deflationary channel. The Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) for manufacturing has continuously remained in the contraction range below 50, total social retail sales growth has stagnated, and the real estate market bubble has begun to burst. Even though some cities have lifted purchase restrictions and banks have lowered down payments to 15%, real estate prices have still fallen by an average of about 30% over the past two years. Consequently, local government land finance has suffered a severe blow.

At the same time, the youth unemployment rate has soared to 18%, and the pressure of local government debt is growing increasingly severe. Faced with such a grim economic situation, relying solely on the slogan of “singing a good economy” is no longer enough to reverse the decline; monetary and fiscal policies must be utilized to alleviate the local fiscal crisis and the youth unemployment problem.

On September 24th, the People’s Bank of China (PBOC) implemented interest rate cuts and reserve requirement ratio cuts, and provided liquidity support to securities, fund, and insurance companies through a repurchase mechanism. These financial institutions exchanged high-liquidity assets, such as government bonds and central bank bills, for the liquidity required by the market, thereby stabilizing the stock market.

These policy measures directly drove the stock market up, creating a round of stock market asset bubble. Listed companies thus gained ample capital liquidity, and local governments can also alleviate debt pressure by selling shares of state-owned enterprises. Individual investors participated in this, gaining asset appreciation while also driving consumer recovery, providing short-term momentum for the economy to escape stagnation.

The Market’s Next Trend#

After a week of trading, funds that entered the market early began realizing profits, leading to the market’s high open and low close today. Simultaneously, the Hong Kong stock market has also started to decline. Does this mean the market euphoria is about to end?

I believe that although the market may experience volatility in the short term, the fundamental driving force of this round of activity—the support from monetary and fiscal policies—has not ended. Therefore, the market’s upward trend may continue. To achieve its economic and policy goals, the government may introduce more easing policies, further increasing support for the market and continuing to drive the stock market higher.

Summary#

The current market prosperity is exciting on the surface, but in the long run, an over-rise detached from economic fundamentals is ultimately difficult to sustain.
