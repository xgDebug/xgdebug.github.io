Everyone can use English

  • 中文

The outcome of merely “studying” without “applying” (which is the reality for most Chinese students) is that even after more than a decade, they are still studying and still useless (this is the reality for most Chinese students).

The reason we “study for a lifetime” yet insist on “never applying it for a lifetime” is because we are afraid of making mistakes. When we make mistakes in childhood, they often lead to punishment. In adulthood, even if there is no punishment from others, there is still the self-deprecation and embarrassment caused by making a mistake. Therefore, many people avoid errors “at all costs.” However, we must know that the process of acquiring knowledge cannot be separated from trial and error. Without trial and error, comprehensive and genuine progress is impossible. So, you must know that making mistakes is normal, and it is even indispensable. When doing something, making mistakes is inevitable. If you are doing something and there isn’t a single mistake, then you aren’t doing something—you are dreaming.

“animals are born equal, but some animals are more equal than others.”

There are many benefits to being able to read English fluently. The word “freedom” basically summarizes the greatest benefit. For example, we can read primary source materials directly, without having to wait a long time to read translations that are often riddled with errors. Translations are often unreliable because translation is truly, truly a difficult job. Often, when a translation is unreliable, it’s not the fault of the translator; at least, a portion of the language is fundamentally untranslatable. If you can read English fluently, you will inevitably laugh when reading a translation—many sentences are translated incorrectly, and you can even reconstruct the original English in your mind based on experience. Not relying on translation is a tremendous liberation. When your English reading reaches a certain level of proficiency, you will naturally feel grateful; it is a gratitude worth a thousand pieces of gold. However, we haven’t reached that point yet. As for the present, whether you believe it or not is up to you.

Not Knowing Yourself#

—Because Chinese nouns do not distinguish between singular and plural, and Chinese verbs do not have tenses. After eliminating these two errors, it is equivalent to solving most of the problems.

“Consistency Relationship”: Subject-verb agreement, tense consistency between the main clause and the subordinate clause, active/passive relationship consistency between non-finite verbs and the logical subject, pronoun reference consistency, consistency of parallel component structure, etc.

As an English user, don’t be misled by English teachers. As a teacher, as part of an educational product, they naturally emphasize “correct,” “accurate,” and “standard”—regardless of whether they themselves are truly “correct,” “accurate,” or “standard.” Sometimes it is quite sad to think about; most teachers have to raise their standards not out of a desire for progress, but merely to meet the “consumer’s” often unreasonable expectations. English teachers are human, and thus most of them have the same psychological limitations or human limitations as most other people. When a person cannot do something, they pretend, and keep pretending until they truly believe it themselves.

Another important concern about not daring to speak English is being ridiculed by those around you. This is indeed annoying. But think about it: would a normal person ridicule a baby’s stumbling steps? No. Would a normal person ridicule a disabled person? No, and they shouldn’t. So look at it: if someone speaks a foreign language with non-standard or unpleasant pronunciation, is it reasonable for them to be ridiculed? If someone speaks a foreign language and carries an accent their whole life, unable to change it, is it reasonable for them to be ridiculed?

“Whether they understood” is not important here. The goal of significantly increasing auditory input is to rebuild speech recognition ability; the focus is not on textual comprehension ability. Babies have…

…already solidified the “Bilingual Speech Filter.” In fact, I highly suspect that various English early education programs consume too much time that could have been used to acquire other skills, thereby limiting the development of the young child’s brain. However, you cannot expect that just by listening to recordings or watching TV, a child will learn a foreign language.

—As long as you start, you will find that it is not difficult, because “even if you don’t understand, it doesn’t matter.” The more you listen, the longer you listen, sooner or later, it won’t be too hard to understand. Of course, even at the beginning, for better results, you can consciously gradually increase the text difficulty, and it is best to combine it with intensive reading. During this period, almost everyone will feel like there is no progress, but this…

Mulling Over (or Deep Thinking)#

The most serious yet most hidden reason for most people failing to learn English might be this: most people have too high an expectation for short-term gains and too low an expectation for long-term gains.

In fact, most people find it difficult to read any sentence fluently in their native language—even though most people believe they can do it. For example, perhaps most people can read any sentence from Reader magazine relatively fluently without any preparation.

But these “quick reading” suggestions are basically treating the symptoms, not the root cause, because they only solve the problem of input speed, not the problem of comprehension speed after input. No matter how fast the input speed is, if the comprehension speed cannot keep up, what good is it? Sometimes you wonder why so many people clearly think in their minds that they want to improve…

  1. Picking up a dictionary and looking up words independently is one of the most direct and effective ways to improve reading comprehension.

  2. Those who don’t look up words—our bodies’ every organ possesses a certain “evolutionary ability” to some degree. If you play the piano for a long time, your fingers become more flexible; if you exercise consistently for a long time, your cardiorespiratory function becomes stronger; if you speak more than others, your tongue becomes more flexible than others.

The biggest difference between English teachers and students might be just one: English teachers must look up words in a dictionary (or other reference books, if there are others), while students have another option besides looking up dictionaries—participating in various courses. What do teachers do in English class? Actually, they mostly just tell the students the words and phrases they looked up the previous night, and the sentences they figured out after thinking about them using the dictionary (and other reference books). And the students? They listen. The students listen, but essentially, they do not participate in the process of reading comprehension.

If you meet a person who doesn’t understand that “love” and “marriage” are not the same, it is best to keep your distance, otherwise, a painful life “is waiting for you not far away”…

If you meet someone who doesn’t understand the difference between “government” and “country,” it is best not to communicate with them easily, otherwise, you might discover endless troubles and calamities because of it…

If you don’t know that “goal” and “plan” are not the same, and you don’t know that you might ultimately fail to achieve your goal because of “sticking to the plan”…

If you don’t know that “going to school” (上学) and “studying” (学习) are not the same, some of them might look down on people who graduated from vocational schools because they have a PhD; conversely, others might hate those with PhDs because they only have a vocational diploma…

If you don’t know the difference between “a person” and “a person’s view,” you might either blindly trust authority or consider yourself to be the absolute authority…

If you don’t know the difference between “Action” and “Reaction,” you might spend your whole life as a slave to your surroundings while remaining completely unconscious…

Many students hate history class; it is actually just that they haven’t understood the important difference between “history” and “a history book”…

Organizing vocabulary. You must know that organizing vocabulary yourself after reading an article is far more efficient than memorizing word lists—unfortunately, most people don’t believe it.

If a fifth-grade student can read independently for 10 minutes every day, they will read 622,000 more words per year than children who don’t read independently…

—Whether it is abstract or concrete. What are adjectives for? You use adjectives to record your perception of the thing you can sense. What about verbs? Verbs are used to record the action emitted by the thing you can sense. So, finally, adverbs? When adverbs modify verbs, they are used to record your perception of the action emitted by the thing you can sense; when adverbs modify adjectives, they are used to record your perception of the perception of the thing you can sense. (This minute’s content seems somewhat convoluted, but as long as you can accurately pause these words and understand them without error, you will be transformed in just one minute.)

“Concise, simple, effective, accurate, specific.” Of course, this is not an iron law; if you have the ability to explain the principle clearly with ornate words, that is not impossible; conversely, telling a moving story with simple language is also possible. But everyone who hopes that their thoughts can be effectively expressed should keep these ten words in mind.
