I am you!

This article introduces six steps to avoid conflict escalation in communication, including calming one's own and others' emotions, understanding the other party's perspective, confirming understanding through restatement (or paraphrasing), using parallel phrasing to express personal views, and collaboratively finding solutions.

Avoid getting caught in the cycle of explanation, threats, and personal attacks.

Step 1#

Calm Your Own Emotions When we feel the impulse to attack the other person internally, we need to try our best to suppress it. For example, we can achieve this through several methods mentioned in the story: · Correcting misjudgments of facts; · Distracting yourself through deep breathing; · Or simply holding back the fist you want to throw; · Or any other method that might work. Every time you feel this impulse flare up during communication with someone, you need to review this step in your mind. Completing this step only takes a few seconds, and it is probably the hardest, yet most important, step. If you don’t have the impulse to attack the other person, that’s naturally even better. You can proceed directly to the next step.

Step 2#

Calm the Other Person’s Emotions If the person you are talking to remains calm, it’s easy, and you can skip this step directly. But what if the other person is not calm? The answer is “do almost nothing,” or rather, “do almost nothing.” Especially don’t say to the other person, “Calm down!” or “Getting angry is useless!” At this time, you should follow the principle of “not contradicting, not judging.” Specifically, how do you achieve this? You can reply to the other person using words like “agree,” “okay,” “yes,” or “that’s right.” These words can convey the message to the other person: “What you are saying, and the way you choose to say it, naturally makes sense to you. Step 2: Calm the Other Person’s Emotions If the person you are talking to remains calm, it’s easy, and you can skip this step directly. But what if the other person is not calm? The answer is “do almost nothing,” or rather, “do almost nothing.” Especially don’t say to the other person, “Calm down!” or “Getting angry is useless!” At this time, you should follow the principle of “not contradicting, not judging.” Specifically, how do you achieve this? You can reply to the other person using words like “agree,” “okay,” “yes,” or “that’s right.” These words can convey the message to the other person: “What you are saying, and the way you choose to say it, naturally makes sense to you. I am willing to discuss this problem.”

By doing this, you will achieve an astonishing effect: the other person’s emotions will initially fluctuate, but then they will gradually stabilize until they slowly calm down. Achieving this step only requires the moment you say, “I agree.” If you successfully control your own emotions and successfully calm the other person’s emotions, you can proceed to the next step.

Step 3#

Try to Understand Others Rather Than Letting Others Be Understood How do you achieve this step? The simplest method is “asking the other person questions.” The most effective question is: “Why do you disagree with my point of view?” After that, you must listen to their answer and try to look at the issue from their perspective, even putting yourself in their shoes and considering their interests. Strive to understand and accept others’ viewpoints, rather than imposing your own viewpoint on them. You must learn to find “consensus” between both parties in the words they use when explaining, and happily accept the other person’s viewpoint. This is a prerequisite for both parties reaching “consensus.” At this time, you should focus more on others’ “interests” (or “selfishness”): make yourself consider the other person’s interests more! Once you understand the other person’s thoughts, the solution will reveal itself, and the disagreement will be resolved easily. But usually, achieving this is not enough; you need to continue to complete the next step…

Step 4#

By Restating the Other Person’s Words to Let Them Understand “That You Have Understood Their Viewpoint” If you want others to listen to your viewpoint, you first need them to speak. Then, use your own words to recount the viewpoint of the other person that you have understood. After that, ask the other person, “Did I get it right?” This will yield an unexpected magical effect—the other person will raise their eyebrows, show a satisfied smile, and exclaim, “That’s right!” Then they will fall silent and listen to you speak. Restating has two benefits: First, you can check whether you truly understood the other person’s viewpoint; Second, it lets your conversational partner know that they have been understood, thereby dispelling the thought of continuing the argument. However, there is a prerequisite for achieving this step—don’t let your previous efforts go down the drain just because you used the wrong word. This word is the keyword for the next step. The use of this word is also an art.

Step 5#

Use Parallel Conjunctions to Present Your Viewpoint, Rather Than Pitting Two Opposing Views Against Each Other How do you achieve this? You can use the following words, for example: “As for me,” “For me,” “At the same time,” “From my perspective,” etc., instead of the extremely stiff “That’s right, but…” Then, after both parties have clearly stated the other person’s viewpoint, ask yourself, “How can the other person’s needs be met while simultaneously achieving my own goals?” By doing so, you can utilize the intelligence of both parties to seek a solution. Next, we begin to enter the sixth step.

Step 6#

Propose Solutions Using Possible Win-Win Scenarios If we cannot find a feasible solution ourselves, try asking others if they have any good ideas. Then, we can discuss it together. If we cannot find any win-win method, we can use a compromise approach to solve the problem. People are often willing to accept a compromise because it at least establishes a friendly cooperative relationship. So, what if even a compromise solution doesn’t exist? This situation is very rare, but not impossible. At this time, you can negotiate with the other person and spend time together discussing possible ways out. Even if a solution is not ultimately found, at least a good relationship is maintained. And this positive relationship is crucial for achieving future collaboration.
