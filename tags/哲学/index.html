Rule Benefits: Once the lower class ties their self-worth to the grand narrative, the rulers can, at a very low cost (a medal, a slogan), compel them to dedicate their property and even their lives (for example, in war). Rule Benefits: Consumerism causes ordinary people to willingly return the wealth they painstakingly earned back to the capitalists, forming a perfect closed loop; while “Tittytainment” fills the time and brain bandwidth of the lower class with short, quick “dopamine” hits, stripping them of the energy needed for deep thought and resistance. Rule Benefits: When humans are dissatisfied with the status quo, they only think, “I chose the wrong person; I’ll switch next time,” instead of overthrowing the game rules that are actually deeply tied to the interests of the elite groups. Rule Benefits: It strips the victim of the rationality to demand compensation and change the status quo. The higher-ups who truly create the suffering never actively suffer themselves; they merely encourage the lower class to taste the hardship. 6. Blue Star citizens think they have bought a house, but in reality, they have mortgaged their life and labor for the next few decades to the financial institutions. Rule Benefits: It greatly maintains the stability of the system. A Blue Star citizen burdened with a 30-year mortgage is the most docile creature in the entire universe. They dare not quit their jobs, dare not get sick, and dare not resist unreasonable work systems. Debt acts like an invisible collar, forcing them to self-regulate and function as batteries at their workstations every day. 7. More cleverly, the algorithms deliberately create group polarization (such as gender conflict, racial conflict, aesthetic conflict), causing the lower class to tear each other apart and expend energy online. As long as the lower class is hostile toward each other over minor differences, they can never unite to gaze upon the true edifice of the system. 8. It encourages people to live alone, consume alone, and face all risks alone, breaking the entire society down into unconnected “atoms.” Rule Benefits: When the individual is in an atomized state, they have absolutely no bargaining power against massive capital and the state machine. At the same time, loneliness is infinitely amplified, and to alleviate this loneliness, Blue Star citizens must purchase goods and services (pet economy, companionship economy, virtual idols), thus providing profit to the host system once again. 9. Rule Benefits: The system extracts the “golden surplus value” from humans at their most creative and valuable point. The principles of conduct are essentially the art of adaptation. How can one be dogmatic about a single aspect?

The way of navigating life is also the art of adaptation; it cannot be rigidly fixed on one side. Mistakes in the Last Assessment My first speculative attempt, lacked consideration of the factor of project failure (or default). We may need to continuously correct our judgments, because the world is changing. Avoiding Extreme Thinking Being biased toward one side means falling into extreme thinking, believing that a certain strategy or concept can apply forever, or holding an absolute view of certain things. This way of thinking ignores the complexity and diversity of reality. Navigating life and responding to change requires balance; one cannot be too aggressive, nor too conservative. In economic decision-making, behavior biased toward one side may manifest as excessive reliance on a certain policy tool (such as monetary easing or fiscal stimulus), ignoring long-term side effects. Similarly, when individuals invest, if they relentlessly pursue a certain investment model, they may miss other potential opportunities. Winning by Adapting to the Enemy The world is full of uncertainty and variables, whether in politics, economics, or personal life. Facing these changes, rigidly adhering to a fixed strategy or concept will lead to mistakes, or even missed opportunities. Adaptation means adjusting one’s behavior and strategy according to environmental changes, maintaining flexibility and adaptability. The “Winning by Adapting to the Enemy” mentioned in Sun Tzu’s Art of War also emphasizes the importance of responding to change. Whether facing the battlefield or various challenges in life, the ability to adapt to change often determines success or failure. Balance and Moderation The Doctrine of the Mean (Zhong Yong) emphasizes finding balance between extremes. True wisdom lies in knowing when to persist and when to change. This applies to life, career, investment, and politics. Timely adjustments not only avoid the difficulties brought by rigidity but also allow us to better seize new opportunities. “Adaptation” is not blindly following change, but adjusting strategy according to the situation while maintaining core principles. This flexibility allows us to maintain direction while effectively meeting challenges. Open Mindset Responding to change also requires us to maintain an open mindset, always ready to accept new knowledge and new information. Often, success depends on whether we can timely recognize the arrival of change and take corresponding action. Those who always cling to old ideas and are unwilling to accept change are often eliminated during periods of flux. In this age of information explosion, maintaining openness and critical thinking is necessary to avoid being misled and to quickly adapt to new situations and challenges.
